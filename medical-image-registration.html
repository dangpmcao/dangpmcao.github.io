01 / FOUNDATIONSWhat Is Image Registration?

Image registration estimates a spatial transformation that brings a moving image or geometry into alignment with a fixed image or geometry.

Let

\[\fixed:\Omega_F\to\R,\qquad \moving:\Omega_M\to\R\]

be fixed and moving images. A transformation

\[\T_\theta:\Omega_M\to\Omega_F\]

with parameters \(\theta\) maps coordinates from the moving domain to the fixed domain. The abstract registration problem is

\[\boxed{\theta^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{\theta}\Loss\!\left(F,\,M\circ\T_\theta^{-1}\right)}.\]

Here \(F\) and \(M\) need not be raw images: they may be intensities, masks, signed-distance fields, landmarks, point clouds, or learned features.

The central difficulty is that familiar terms from registration papers belong to different conceptual levels:

  • SDF describes a representation of a segmentation.
  • Rigid, affine, B-spline, and diffeomorphic describe transformation models.
  • Dice, NCC, mutual information, and Chamfer distance describe objectives or similarity measures.
  • Adam, L-BFGS, Powell, and SVD describe optimization or solution strategies.
  • ICP combines correspondence estimation, an objective, and an iterative solver.

This motivates the four-layer framework used throughout this article.

02 / MENTAL MODELThe Four-Layer Mental Model

A useful way to understand almost any registration method is to ask four questions in order:

Layer 1: Representation / Registration SignalWhat information is being matched?
↓
Layer 2: Transformation ModelHow may the moving object move or deform?
↓
Layer 3: Matching Objective / Similarity MetricHow is alignment quality measured?
↓
Layer 4: Optimizer / SolverHow are the best parameters found?
\[\boxed{\text{Representation}\rightarrow\text{Transformation}\rightarrow\text{Objective}\rightarrow\text{Optimizer}}\]

A registration method can therefore be specified as a tuple:

\[\boxed{(\text{signal},\ \text{transform},\ \text{metric},\ \text{solver})}.\]

For example, \((\text{multi-label SDF},\ \text{rigid},\ \text{SDF loss},\ \text{L-BFGS})\), or \((\text{surface points},\ \text{rigid},\ \text{nearest-point distance},\ \text{ICP/SVD})\).

03 / LAYER 1Representation / Registration Signal

Layer 1 asks: What information from the fixed and moving data is actually used to establish alignment?

Intensity-Based Registration

Intensity-based methods operate directly on voxel or pixel values:

\[F(x)=I_F(x),\qquad M(x)=I_M(x).\]

Typical applications include CT–CT, MRI–MRI, PET–CT, and sometimes CT–MRI or CT–US when a suitable multimodal metric is used. For a transformed moving image,

\[I_M^T(x)=I_M\!\left(T^{-1}(x)\right),\]

registration compares \(I_F(x)\) with \(I_M^T(x)\) over a region of interest. This avoids segmentation or landmark extraction, but multimodal cases are difficult because intensities need not share the same physical meaning.

Feature-Based Registration

Feature-based registration extracts a representation \(f(I)\) from each image and registers those features instead of raw intensity. Examples include anatomical landmarks, keypoints, edges, contours, vessel bifurcations, surfaces, segmentation masks, signed-distance fields, and learned feature maps.

\[\boxed{\text{Registration signal}\in\{\text{intensity-based},\ \text{feature/geometry-based}\}}\]

Segmentation-, landmark-, and surface-based registration are commonly treated as subclasses of feature-based registration, although terminology varies.

Segmentation Masks

A binary segmentation mask is \(M(x)\in\{0,1\}\). For multiple labels,

\[M_c(x)\in\{0,1\},\qquad c\in\mathcal C,\]

where \(\mathcal C\) might contain the LV, RV, LA, RA, and myocardium. Registration may optimize a mask-based objective such as Dice, or convert masks to smoother representations such as SDFs.

Signed-Distance Fields (SDFs)

Given a segmented region \(\Omega\) with boundary \(\partial\Omega\), a signed-distance field is commonly defined as

\[\phi(x)=\sdf(x)=\begin{cases}-d(x,\partial\Omega),&x\in\Omega,\\0,&x\in\partial\Omega,\\+d(x,\partial\Omega),&x\notin\Omega,\end{cases}\qquad d(x,\partial\Omega)=\min_{y\in\partial\Omega}\|x-y\|_2.\]

The sign convention may be reversed, but the geometry is unchanged: each point stores its distance to the surface. Unlike a binary mask, an SDF supplies a spatially smooth boundary signal even when two masks do not overlap.

\[\widetilde\phi(x)=\frac{\operatorname{clip}(\phi(x),-d_{\max},d_{\max})}{d_{\max}}.\]

For example, \(d_{\max}=15\,\mathrm{mm}\) limits the influence of very distant voxels.

Surfaces and Point Clouds

A segmentation can be converted to a surface and sampled as a point cloud:

\[S=\partial\Omega,\qquad P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_N\}\subset\R^3.\]

This representation is natural for ICP, Chamfer-distance registration, and point-to-plane optimization.

Landmarks

For corresponding landmarks \(Q=\{q_i\}_{i=1}^N\) and \(P=\{p_i\}_{i=1}^N\), registration may minimize

\[\Loss(T)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|p_i-T(q_i)\|_2^2.\]

Unlike ICP, the correspondence \(q_i\leftrightarrow p_i\) is already known.

Learned Features

A neural feature extractor \(f_\psi\) may map images into feature space:

\[F(x)=f_\psi(I_F)(x),\qquad M(x)=f_\psi(I_M)(x).\]

This is useful when appearance varies strongly across modalities.

04 / LAYER 2Transformation Models

Layer 2 asks: What spatial changes are allowed?

Translation-Only Registration

\[T(x)=x+t,\qquad t=\begin{bmatrix}t_x&t_y&t_z\end{bmatrix}^{\!T}.\]

In 3D, translation has \(\boxed{3\ \text{DOF}}\).

Rigid Registration

\[\boxed{T(x)=Rx+t},\qquad R\in SO(3),\quad R^TR=I,\quad\det(R)=1,\quad t\in\R^3.\]

A common parameterization is \(\theta=(\theta_x,\theta_y,\theta_z,t_x,t_y,t_z)\), giving \(\boxed{6\ \text{DOF}}\). Rigid motion preserves lengths, angles, shape, and scale; every point receives the same global transform.

Rotation matrices

\[R_x(\alpha)=\begin{bmatrix}1&0&0\\0&\cos\alpha&-\sin\alpha\\0&\sin\alpha&\cos\alpha\end{bmatrix},\quad R_y(\beta)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\beta&0&\sin\beta\\0&1&0\\-\sin\beta&0&\cos\beta\end{bmatrix},\]
\[R_z(\gamma)=\begin{bmatrix}\cos\gamma&-\sin\gamma&0\\\sin\gamma&\cos\gamma&0\\0&0&1\end{bmatrix},\qquad R=R_z(\gamma)R_y(\beta)R_x(\alpha).\]

Rotation order must always be stated because matrix multiplication is not commutative.

Similarity Registration

\[T(x)=sRx+t.\]

Three rotations, three translations, and one global scale give \(\boxed{7\ \text{DOF}}\).

Affine Registration

\[\boxed{T(x)=Ax+t},\qquad A\in\R^{3\times3}.\]

A full 3D affine transform has nine matrix parameters plus three translations, or \(\boxed{12\ \text{DOF}}\). It permits translation, rotation, isotropic and anisotropic scaling, and shear.

\[\boxed{\text{translation}\subset\text{rigid}\subset\text{similarity}\subset\text{affine}}.\]

Deformable / Non-Rigid Registration

\[\boxed{T(x)=x+u(x)},\qquad u(x)=\begin{bmatrix}u_x(x)\\u_y(x)\\u_z(x)\end{bmatrix}.\]

Unlike a rigid or affine transform, a displacement field allows different locations to move differently.

B-Spline Free-Form Deformation

B-spline registration is a transformation model, not a metric or optimizer. Put control-point displacements \(\Phi_{ijk}\in\R^3\) on a lattice and interpolate them:

\[\boxed{u(x,y,z)=\sum_{i,j,k}B_i(x)B_j(y)B_k(z)\Phi_{ijk}},\qquad T(x)=x+u(x).\]

Cubic B-splines provide local control and smoothness. Coarse control-point spacing produces smooth, low-frequency deformation; a fine grid adds local flexibility but increases the risk of overfitting or unrealistic deformation.

Dense Displacement Fields

\[T(x)=x+u(x),\qquad u(x)\in\R^3\ \text{for every voxel }x.\]

This is a very high-dimensional model and usually needs smoothness regularization.

Optical Flow

Optical flow estimates spatially varying motion, often under brightness constancy:

\[I(x,t)\approx I(x+u(x),t+1).\]

Demons Registration

\[u_{k+1}=u_k+\Delta u_k.\]

Demons methods iteratively estimate an update and smooth or regularize the field. Variants include classical, symmetric, and diffeomorphic Demons.

Diffeomorphic Registration

A diffeomorphic transformation is smooth and invertible with a smooth inverse. A common local condition is

\[\boxed{\det J_T(x)>0},\]

which discourages folding and supports topology-preserving deformation. Important families include LDDMM, SyN, stationary velocity field methods, and diffeomorphic Demons.

LDDMM

Large Deformation Diffeomorphic Metric Mapping models a time-dependent flow:

\[\frac{d\phi_t(x)}{dt}=v_t(\phi_t(x)),\qquad \phi_0(x)=x.\]

The final transformation is \(\phi_1\); regularity is controlled through constraints on \(v_t\).

SyN

Symmetric Normalization maps both fixed and moving images toward a common intermediate space instead of assigning all deformation to only one image.

05 / LAYER 3Matching Objectives and Similarity Metrics

Layer 3 asks: Given a candidate transformation, how do we quantify whether the alignment is good?

Sum of Squared Differences / Mean Squared Error

\[\Loss_{SSD}(T)=\sum_{x\in\Omega}\left[I_F(x)-I_M(T^{-1}(x))\right]^2,\qquad \Loss_{MSE}(T)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\Loss_{SSD}(T).\]

These metrics assume corresponding structures have comparable intensities.

Normalized Cross-Correlation

\[\operatorname{NCC}(F,M)=\frac{\sum_x(F(x)-\bar F)(M(x)-\bar M)}{\sqrt{\sum_x(F(x)-\bar F)^2}\sqrt{\sum_x(M(x)-\bar M)^2}}.\]

Registration maximizes NCC or minimizes \(-\operatorname{NCC}\). Local NCC applies the same idea over windows and is more robust to spatially varying intensity changes.

Mutual Information

\[\operatorname{MI}(F,M)=H(F)+H(M)-H(F,M)=\sum_{f,m}p(f,m)\log\frac{p(f,m)}{p(f)p(m)}.\]

MI measures statistical dependence instead of numerical equality, making it useful for multimodal registration.

Dice Similarity

\[\operatorname{Dice}(A,B)=\frac{2|A\cap B|}{|A|+|B|},\qquad \Loss_{Dice}=1-\operatorname{Dice}.\]

Dice is intuitive for overlap but gives a weak signal when masks begin far apart and do not overlap.

SDF Loss

\[\boxed{\Loss_{SDF}(T)=\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{x\in\Omega}\left[\phi_F(x)-\phi_M(T^{-1}(x))\right]^2}.\]

The distance fields may first be clipped and normalized. For multiple structures,

\[\boxed{\Loss_{multi}(T)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}w_c\Loss_c(T)},\qquad w_c=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}.\]

Balanced weights stop a large structure such as the LV from dominating a smaller atrium merely because it contains more voxels.

Point-to-Point Distance

\[\Loss_{p2p}(T)=\sum_i\|p_i-T(q_i)\|_2^2.\]

This is the classical rigid ICP objective after correspondence assignment.

Point-to-Plane Distance

\[\boxed{\Loss_{p2pl}(T)=\sum_i\left[n_i^T(T(q_i)-p_i)\right]^2}.\]

It penalizes error along each fixed point’s local surface normal instead of the full Euclidean distance.

Chamfer Distance

\[D_{Chamfer}(P,Q)=\frac{1}{|P|}\sum_{p\in P}\min_{q\in Q}\|p-q\|_2^2+\frac{1}{|Q|}\sum_{q\in Q}\min_{p\in P}\|q-p\|_2^2.\]

The symmetric Chamfer distance penalizes mismatch in both directions.

Hausdorff Distance

\[D_H(P,Q)=\max\left\{\sup_{p\in P}\inf_{q\in Q}\|p-q\|,\sup_{q\in Q}\inf_{p\in P}\|q-p\|\right\}.\]

It measures the worst mismatch and is consequently more sensitive to outliers than average surface distances.

06 / LAYER 4Optimizers and Solvers

Layer 4 asks: How do we find the transformation parameters that optimize the chosen objective?

Gradient Descent

\[\theta_{k+1}=\theta_k-\eta\nabla_\theta\Loss(\theta_k),\]

where \(\eta\) is the learning rate.

Adam

Adam maintains moving estimates of the gradient’s first and second moments. It is common in differentiable registration pipelines and neural registration models.

L-BFGS

L-BFGS is a quasi-Newton optimizer that approximates curvature from a limited history of gradient updates. It is often effective for smooth objectives with a moderate number of parameters.

Powell and Derivative-Free Search

Powell’s method does not need analytical gradients. Derivative-free methods are useful when interpolation, resampling, or metric computation makes gradients difficult or unreliable.

Gauss–Newton and Levenberg–Marquardt

For nonlinear least squares,

\[\Loss(\theta)=\tfrac12\|r(\theta)\|^2,\qquad H\approx J^TJ,\]

where \(J\) is the residual Jacobian. Levenberg–Marquardt adds damping:

\[(J^TJ+\lambda I)\Delta\theta=-J^Tr.\]

Closed-Form SVD for Rigid Point Matching

For paired point sets \((q_i,p_i)\), first compute and subtract the centroids:

\[\bar q=\frac1N\sum_iq_i,\quad \bar p=\frac1N\sum_ip_i,\qquad \widetilde q_i=q_i-\bar q,\quad \widetilde p_i=p_i-\bar p.\]

Construct the cross-covariance and take its singular value decomposition:

\[H=\sum_i\widetilde q_i\widetilde p_i^T,\qquad H=U\Sigma V^T.\]

After correcting a reflection if necessary,

\[\boxed{R=VU^T},\qquad\boxed{t=\bar p-R\bar q}.\]

This single solve estimates all six rigid degrees of freedom jointly from all correspondences.

07 / ALGORITHMICP: Why It Does Not Fit into Only One Layer

Core Idea

Iterative Closest Point registers point sets or surfaces. Classical rigid ICP alternates between estimating nearest-neighbor correspondences, solving one rigid transformation from all correspondences, applying that transformation, and repeating until convergence. Its representation is usually a point cloud or surface, while ICP itself spans the matching and solving stages.

One ICP Iteration

Let \(Q=\{q_1,\ldots,q_N\}\) be the moving surface, \(P=\{p_1,\ldots,p_M\}\) the fixed surface, and \(T_k(x)=R_kx+t_k\) the current transform. First transform every moving point:

\[q_i'=T_k(q_i).\]

Then assign its closest fixed point:

\[\boxed{p_i=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{p\in P}\|q_i'-p\|_2}.\]

Estimate one global incremental rigid transform from all pairs:

\[\Delta T_k=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_T\sum_i\|p_i-T(q_i')\|_2^2,\qquad \boxed{T_{k+1}=\Delta T_k\circ T_k}.\]

How the Six DOFs Are Updated

Rigid ICP does not give each point its own motion. Every correspondence contributes evidence about one global parameter vector:

\[\theta=\begin{bmatrix}\theta_x&\theta_y&\theta_z&t_x&t_y&t_z\end{bmatrix}^{\!T}.\]

For point-to-plane ICP, a small-motion update uses

\[\xi=\begin{bmatrix}\omega_x&\omega_y&\omega_z&t_x&t_y&t_z\end{bmatrix}^{\!T},\qquad r_i\approx J_i\xi-b_i.\]

Stacking all residuals gives \(J\xi\approx b\) and

\[\boxed{\xi^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_\xi\|J\xi-b\|_2^2}.\]

Many points “vote” for one six-parameter motion.

ICP Requires Good Initialization

ICP is local. Under poor alignment, a geometrically nearest point may be anatomically wrong. Robust pipelines therefore use

\[\boxed{\text{coarse initialization}\rightarrow\text{ICP refinement}},\]

where initialization may use centroids, PCA, landmarks, a global search, or a previous stage.

Label-Aware ICP

Naively merging anatomical point sets can create invalid correspondences such as \(LV_{US}\to Myocardium_{CT}\). Label-aware matching instead enforces \(LV_{US}\leftrightarrow LV_{CT}\), \(RV_{US}\leftrightarrow RV_{CT}\), and so on, while optimizing one shared transformation:

\[\Loss(T)=\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}w_c\frac{1}{N_c}\sum_{i=1}^{N_c}d\!\left(T(q_i^c),P^c\right)^2.\]

08 / COMPARISONSDF Registration Versus ICP

SDF registration and ICP can both use segmentation geometry, but they operate differently.

AspectSDF-based registrationICP
RepresentationVolumetric signed-distance fieldSurface or point cloud
CorrespondenceNo explicit point correspondencesExplicit nearest-neighbor correspondences
Typical objectiveDifference between distance fieldsPoint-to-point or point-to-plane distance
TransformRigid, affine, B-spline, etc.Usually rigid, though variants exist
OptimizationGeneral numerical optimizerAlternate correspondence and transform solve
Far from overlapSDF still provides a graded signalNearest neighbors may be anatomically wrong
Domain3D voxel fieldSurface samples
Multiple labelsOne SDF channel per labelNeeds label-aware matching or weighting
InitializationImportant, but the objective can be smootherOften highly sensitive
Typical roleCoarse-to-medium geometric registrationLocal surface refinement

A hybrid strategy can be sensible:

\[\boxed{\begin{aligned}&\text{centroid/PCA initialization}\rightarrow\text{multi-label SDF rigid registration}\\[-1mm]&\hspace{22mm}\rightarrow\text{label-aware ICP refinement}.\end{aligned}}\]

The ICP step is optional and must be validated: a lower surface distance does not automatically mean higher Dice or better anatomical correspondence.

09 / TAXONOMYRegistration Taxonomy Beyond the Four Layers

The four layers describe how a method is built. Registration problems are also classified along several independent axes.

By Transformation Scope

  • Global: one transform applies to the whole object, such as rigid or affine.
  • Local / deformable: motion varies spatially, such as a B-spline FFD or dense field.

By Modality

  • Mono-modal: CT–CT, MRI–MRI, or US–US.
  • Multi-modal: CT–MRI, PET–CT, or CT–US.

By Subject

  • Intra-subject: the same patient across times, phases, or modalities.
  • Inter-subject: different patients.
  • Atlas-to-subject: a population template registered to an individual.

By Dimensionality

\[2D\to2D,\qquad2D\to3D,\qquad3D\to3D,\qquad3D+t\ \text{or other 4D registration}.\]

By Temporal Structure

For a cardiac sequence \(I(x,t)\), \(t=0,\ldots,T-1\), temporal registration may estimate \(\phi_{0\to t}\) from one reference phase to every other phase. Sequential transformations compose as

\[\boxed{\phi_{0\to2}=\phi_{1\to2}\circ\phi_{0\to1}}.\]

11 / WORKED EXAMPLERigid Surface Registration with ICP

Assume a moving ultrasound surface contains points \(q_i\) and a fixed CT surface contains points \(p_j\).

Step 1: Initialization

\[t_0=\bar p-\bar q,\qquad T_0(x)=x+t_0.\]

Step 2: Correspondence Assignment

\[p_i=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{p\in P}\|T_k(q_i)-p\|.\]

Step 3: Rigid Solve

\[(R_\Delta,t_\Delta)=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_{R,t}\sum_i\|p_i-(RT_k(q_i)+t)\|^2.\]

Step 4: Transform Composition

\[T_{k+1}=\Delta T_k\circ T_k.\]

Step 5: Convergence

\[|\Loss_{k+1}-\Loss_k|<\epsilon\]

or stop when the transform update is sufficiently small. The result is still one global rigid transformation, not one transform per point.

12 / WORKED EXAMPLEMulti-Label SDF Rigid Registration

Assume five classes:

\[\mathcal C=\{LV,RV,LA,RA,Myo\}.\]

For every class, convert fixed and moving masks into normalized SDFs \(\phi_F^c\) and \(\phi_M^c\), and choose a rigid transform

\[T_\theta(x)=R(\theta_x,\theta_y,\theta_z)x+t.\]

A balanced loss is

\[\Loss(\theta)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal C|}\sum_{c\in\mathcal C}\frac{1}{|\Omega_c|}\sum_{x\in\Omega_c}\left[\phi_F^c(x)-\phi_M^c(T_\theta^{-1}(x))\right]^2.\]

Then solve

\[\boxed{\theta^*=\operatorname*{arg\,min}_\theta\Loss(\theta)}\]

with L-BFGS, Powell, or another suitable optimizer. The six resulting numbers \((\theta_x,\theta_y,\theta_z,t_x,t_y,t_z)\) are applied consistently to the full ultrasound volume and every associated label.

13 / WORKED EXAMPLEB-Spline Deformable Registration

Suppose the fixed and moving images are two phases of a cardiac CT sequence. Because the heart changes shape through the cardiac cycle, a rigid transform is insufficient.

Place control points \(\Phi_{ijk}\) on a 3D lattice. The displacement field and transform are

\[u(x,y,z)=\sum_{i,j,k}B_i(x)B_j(y)B_k(z)\Phi_{ijk},\qquad T(x)=x+u(x).\]

An intensity-based objective might be

\[\Loss(T)=-\operatorname{LNCC}\!\left(I_F,I_M\circ T^{-1}\right)+\lambda\mathcal R(u),\qquad \mathcal R(u)=\int_\Omega\|\nabla u(x)\|_F^2\,dx.\]

The same four-layer pattern remains:

\[\boxed{\text{CT intensity}+\text{B-spline deformation}+\text{LNCC + regularization}+\text{numerical optimizer}}.\]
